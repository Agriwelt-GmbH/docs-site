What Agrigate is

Agrigate in one page — a gateway that talks to the sensors of the seeder and the Hectare app on an iPad or iPhone in place of the cab monitor.

Agrigate is a seeding monitoring system made by Agriwelt. It watches the seed and fertilizer flow in every row of a seed drill or planter and raises an alarm when a row stops, falls behind or sows wrong. It has two parts:

  • The gateway. A small unit in the cab, connected to the sensor line of the seeder. It reads every sensor on the line and passes what it reads to the tablet by Bluetooth. It has no screen of its own. In the app it is called the “Bridge”.
  • The Hectare app on an iPad or iPhone. It shows the rows, judges them, raises the alarms, keeps the statistics and sets up the gateway and the sensors.
IMPORTANT

This is a pre-release version. The gateway and the app are still changing. These pages describe only what the app and the gateway do today. A function that is not described here should not be relied on.

What replaces what

With a Hectare monitor With Agrigate
The monitor in the cab, wired to the sensor line The gateway in the cab, wired to the sensor line
The monitor's screen and buttons The Hectare app on an iPad or iPhone, linked to the gateway by Bluetooth
The monitor's buzzer The tablet's speaker — the alarm sounds through the tablet

The seed sensors, the HUB-8 hub and the tramline actuators on the seeder are the same Agriwelt devices that work with the Hectare monitors. The app shows them the way the Hectare monitors do: the same bar colours, the same alarm window, the same table and statistics.

What it does today

  • Seed and fertilizer rows. One bar per row, in seeds per second (Flow) or seeds per metre (Hybrid, Precision). See The rows screen.
  • Alarms by row number. A silent, clogged or faulty sensor, a row that does not sow, a row below or above the rate, doubles and skips. See The alarm window.
  • Statistics of five fields — area, sown and not-sown area, quality. See Statistics.
  • The devices of the machine on the HUB-8 hub: hoppers, fans, metering units, work position, tractor inputs, flowmeter. See HUB-8 inputs.
  • Tramline: bouts and rhythm counted by the app, tramline actuators watched. See Tramline.
  • Updating the gateway from the app. See Update the gateway.

Every alarm and fault has a three-digit service code. See How service codes work.

How the parts connect

The path of a reading from the sensor in the seed tube to the screen of the iPad — sensor line, gateway, Bluetooth, app — and what each part knows.

A reading travels this way:

  1. The sensor in the seed tube counts the seeds of its row.
  2. The sensor line on the seeder carries the readings of all sensors, the HUB-8 and the tramline actuators to the cab. Every device on the line has its own number; for a seed sensor it belongs to its row.
  3. The gateway at the cab end of the sensor line asks every device in turn and collects the answers.
  4. Bluetooth carries them from the gateway to the tablet, about twice a second.
  5. The Hectare app draws the rows, judges them against the rate and raises the alarms.

What each part knows

Part Knows Does not know
Sensor Its own count and its own faults Anything about the other rows
Gateway Which devices are on the line, in which order, and what each one answers. It keeps the machine profile the app writes to it. What a HUB-8 input means — a hopper, a fan, the work position. That lives in the app.
App Everything you see: the rate, the control line, the alarms, the statistics, the meaning of every device —

So the judgement is made in the app: the alarm delay, the share of rows that must sow, the tolerance — all these are set in the app. See Control and Alarms settings.

One gateway, one tablet

A gateway talks to one tablet at a time. The app remembers its gateway and connects only to it from then on; when several gateways are in range, it asks which one is yours. See First connection to the gateway.

When the link is lost

When Bluetooth drops, or the gateway stops sending, the app dims the screen — every figure on it is old — and after 3 seconds opens the alarm window “Bridge link lost”. The window closes by itself when the gateway answers again. See code 601.

Mount and connect the gateway

Where the gateway goes, what it is connected to, and what this pre-release version does not describe yet about cables and power.

Where the gateway goes

The gateway goes in the cab, close to where the iPad or iPhone is mounted. The tablet reaches the gateway by Bluetooth: the shorter the distance, the steadier the link. Every message of the app about a lost or broken link asks you to keep the tablet near the gateway.

What it is connected to

The gateway takes the place of the Hectare monitor at the cab end of the sensor line. It is connected to two things:

  • the sensor line of the seeder — the same line of seed sensors, HUB-8 and tramline actuators that a Hectare monitor would be connected to;
  • the power of the tractor.

The sensor line on the seeder itself — the sensors one after another in a chain, the HUB-8 where one is used — is laid out as for a Hectare monitor.

IMPORTANT

This pre-release version does not describe the cables, the connectors and the power connection of the gateway. They are not final. Have the gateway connected by Agriwelt or by a fitter who has the cable set and the instructions for your machine. Do not connect the gateway to a socket or a cable meant for another device.

Check after connecting

The connection is right when the app, with the gateway powered:

  1. finds the gateway and connects to it — see First connection to the gateway;
  2. shows “Sensors on the bus now” in “Settings” › “Machine” with the number of devices you expect on the seeder — see Set up the machine profile;
  3. shows every row of the machine on the rows screen, none of them with a red ✕ — see Bar colours.

First connection to the gateway

How the Hectare app finds the gateway the first time, how to pick yours when several are in range, and how to change or forget it later.

The app finds the gateway by itself. You only pick it when more than one gateway is in range.

  1. Power the gateway and keep the tablet in the cab, close to it.

  2. Open the Hectare app. The first time, iPadOS asks whether the app may use Bluetooth. Allow it — without Bluetooth the app cannot reach the gateway.

  3. The app looks for the gateway. A red capsule in the middle of the screen says “Looking for the bridge…”, and the row pages say “The bridge sends the row count once connected.”

  4. One gateway in range: after a few seconds the app connects to it by itself and remembers it. The capsule shows “Connecting to” and the name of the gateway, then disappears.

    Several gateways in range: the list “Choose bridge” opens. Every gateway is a line with its name, its signal bars and its signal strength; the strongest — usually the nearest — is at the top. Tap the gateway of your seeder.

  5. The rows of the machine appear on the rows screen. If they do not match your seeder, set the machine profile: Set up the machine profile.

From now on the app connects only to this gateway — it is “My bridge” in “Settings” › “Machine” › “Bridge”. If the remembered gateway is not found for a few seconds while others are in range, the list “Choose bridge” opens again.

The bridge section in Settings

“Settings” › “Machine”, section “Bridge”:

Line What it is
The first line The link: a green dot and the name of the gateway when connected, or what the app is doing, with the signal strength.
“My bridge” The remembered gateway, or “not chosen”. Tap it to open the list “Choose bridge” and pick another one.
“Reconnect” Drops the link and connects again to the same gateway.
“Forget bridge” The app forgets the gateway, disconnects and offers the list again. It asks first: “The app will disconnect and let you choose the bridge again.” — “Forget” or “No”.

Set up the machine profile

Tell the app how many seed rows and fertilizer sensors the machine has and, for Hybrid and Precision, its working width; the app writes the profile to the gateway by itself.

The machine profile tells the gateway which sensors to read and the app how to draw them: how many seed rows, how many fertilizer sensors, and for the Hybrid and Precision modes the working width. It is kept in the gateway, so another tablet connected to the same gateway reads the same profile.

  1. Open “Settings” › “Machine”. The section “Machine profile” opens at the top.

  2. Set “Rows” — the number of seed rows — with − / +.

  3. Set “Fertilizer sensors” — the number of fertilizer rows with a sensor, or “none”.

  4. Wait a moment. There is no Save button: after a short pause the app writes the profile to the gateway. The status line under the numbers says “writing the profile to the bridge…” and then “profile written to the bridge at” with the time.

  5. Compare “Sensors on the bus now” with what is on the seeder: it counts every device that answers on the sensor line.

  6. Hybrid and Precision only: set “Width, cm” — the working width. “Row spacing” under it is worked out by the app: the width divided by the rows of the group.

When the profile is not written

The status line says What it means What to do
“bridge busy: seeding under way” The gateway does not take a new profile while seed flows. Stop the seeder and change the number again.
“too many rows for the bridge (up to” … The profile is larger than the gateway takes. Reduce rows or devices. See code 902.
Any other message with a code The gateway refused the profile. See 9xx · Setup answers of the gateway.

Width and statistics

The area and the row spacing come from the width. When the active field already has statistics, a new width asks first: “Statistics must be erased!” — “Yes” starts the statistics of the active field from zero, “No” keeps the old width.

The fertilizer rows come after the seed rows. With fertilizer sensors in the profile, the rows screen shows two bands: seed on top, fertilizer below. See The rows screen.

The rows screen

The main working page — one bar per row, the control line or the rate, the rate column at the right of every band, and the tiles at the bottom.

“Rows” is the main working page and, by default, the home page. Every row of the machine is a bar that grows up from the bottom line; its height is the flow of its row, its colour is the judgement.

Without fertilizer sensors, up to 20 seed rows stand in one band; more stand in two bands, the first half on top.

When the band is not under control

Control starts when enough rows sow within the norm for 3 seconds running — the share is set in “Share of seeding rows”. When seed flows but too few rows are within the norm, the band shows the blinking yellow capsule “NOT UNDER CONTROL” with its rows, and the buzzer sounds. No window opens. Bar colours and the three reds

What every colour, hatching and sign of a row's bar means on the rows screen, and the three different reds.

In the cab you read the colour, not the text. Every bar of the rows screen takes one of the looks below.

The bar itself

What you see What it means
Green bar The row sows within the norm.
Yellow bar The row sows below the control line (Flow) or below the tolerance band (Hybrid, Precision).
Blue bar The row sows above the tolerance band. Only in Hybrid and Precision.
Yellow and blue narrow bars on both sides of a green one Skips (yellow, left) and doubles (blue, right) of the row. Only in Precision, with “Skips & doubles” switched on.

The three reds

What you see What it means What to do
Bright red, blinking, with a red ✕ The sensor has never answered since the gateway was switched on. Check the sensor's plug and cable. Code 101
Grey background with a red ✕ The sensor answered, then went silent. Check the sensor's plug and cable. Code 101
Dark red, blinking The sensor is there, but no seed passes it. Check the metering unit and the seed tube. Code 202

A bright red column blinking with a “?” means that the device on this row's number is not a seed sensor. Code 106

Other looks

What you see What it means
White column with a black “!” The row is taken off control: it raises no alarms and does not count toward the share of seeding rows. The row menu
Grey hatching The row is in the tramline and should not sow. Tramline
Red hatching over the grey The row sows although it is in the tramline. Code 206
A “?” on top of an empty column Nothing is known about the row yet.
A bar filling slowly with a yellow “?” The gateway is sending the sensor a new setting, for example a new “Fraction”. The rows do this one after another; it takes a few seconds each.

A sensor that reports a fault — clogged, light struck, faulty — keeps its bar; the reason comes in the alarm window. 1xx · Row sensor faults

The row menu opens from the rows screen — press and hold a bar, slide to the row and lift the finger, or tap a band and then a row — and from the table, by tapping a line. Its title is “Row” with the number, or “Fertilizer · row” for a fertilizer row. “Done” closes it.

What you can do

To do this Do this What happens
Stop watching a row whose fault you know “Disable control” The row stays on screen white with a “!”, raises no alarms and does not count toward the share of seeding rows.

The tiles at the bottom

Under “Rows” and under “Field” stands a strip of tiles. Each tile shows one value: a word on top, the value in large figures, the unit, and an icon in a ring. The ring's colour is the state: green — normal, red — alarm (the ring blinks), yellow — attention, grey — no data or not answering.

The strip is there when the machine has something to show in it: a seed group in Hybrid or Precision, or devices on the HUB-8. A Flow machine without devices has no strip.

Choose what a place shows

The strip has as many places as fit across the screen: 3 on an iPhone held upright, up to 8 on an iPad held sideways.

  1. Press and hold a tile for half a second. The list “Choose value” opens under it: every value the machine can show, with its icon and current value; the one in this place is marked.

The place at the right is also the alarm place. When a device that is in no place of the strip goes into alarm, its tile stands there with a blinking ring until the alarm is over; a tap opens its page. While it is there, holding it opens no choice — the alarm is not hidden by a choice.

The rate sheet

The rate sheet shows the rate in seeds per metre in large figures and the population in thousand seeds per hectare under it. Change it with − / + (hold for steps of ten), or tap a number and type it. Nothing reaches the gateway until you press “Accept”; swiping the sheet down leaves the rate as it was.

Statistics

The statistics of five fields — distance, area, time, sown and not-sown area, quality, population — and how to select a field or reset its statistics.

“Statistics” keeps the record of the work in five fields. One of them is active: everything the machine sows is counted into it.

What the “Seeding” block shows depends on the control mode of the group. In Precision you get the quality bar, the seeds sown, population and rate. In Flow and Hybrid you get the three amounts “Sown”, “Not sown” and “Unknown”. With two groups in Precision a page switch in the block's title shows the other group.

Select a field or reset it

  1. Use ◄ ► to bring the field you want onto the screen.

  2. Tap … at the right of the title.

  3. Choose:

    • “Select field” — this field becomes the active one. If it already holds statistics, the app first tells you “Data must be erased to select this field!”: “Yes” makes it active with its counters from zero.
    • “Reset statistics” — the app asks “Are you sure?”; “Yes” starts the statistics of this field from zero.

“Select field” is offered only when the field on screen is not the active one.

IMPORTANT

A reset cannot be undone. The statistics of the field are gone. Changing the working width with statistics in the active field resets them too — the app asks first. See Set up the machine profile.

The field picture

The seeder drawn in perspective with every row painting the ground behind it in the colour of its bar — the last metres of every row at a glance.

“Field” draws the seeder as you would see it from the cab, standing on a field that moves under it. Every seed row is a column on the bar of the seeder — the same bar as on “Rows”, with its height and colour. Behind every row the ground is painted in the colour the row shows, and the painted strip runs away to the horizon at the speed of the machine. So you see the last metres of every row at a glance: a row that stopped leaves a gap that moves away from the seeder.

The picture is drawn from what the rows and the speed show at this moment. It is not a map: nothing is stored, and it has no GNSS track.

What you can do

The alarm window

When the app opens the alarm window, what the window shows, how rows join it, how to close it, and how the buzzer and the alarm delay work.

An alarm is a window that opens over whatever page is on screen, with the buzzer. It tells you that a row, a device of the machine or the link to the gateway has a fault.

For a row the window also names the sensor and the rows:

“Seed sensor:” or “Fertilizer sensor:” tells you which group, and the row numbers follow — #4,12–14 means rows 4, 12, 13 and 14. For a tramline actuator the window says “Tramline actuator” and its number.

How the window behaves

  • The fault must hold for the alarm delay before the window opens — 3 seconds unless set otherwise in “Settings” › “Control and Alarms” › “Alarm delay” (2 to 20 seconds). A device that is not a seed sensor (“Wrong device type!”) is reported at once.
  • The same alarm in another row joins the open window: its number is added. A different alarm waits until you close this one, then opens.
  • A closed alarm does not come back while its fault lasts. It shows again only after the fault has gone and returned.
  • The link to the gateway comes first. When the link has been down for 3 seconds, the window “Bridge link lost” replaces any other window: every figure on the screen is old. It closes by itself when the gateway answers again.
  • Closing the window does not repair the fault. Active alarms stay in the list behind the bell — see Moving around the app.

The buzzer

The buzzer sounds while an alarm window is open: a short tone every two seconds, through the tablet's speaker. “Close” stops it. While a band is “NOT UNDER CONTROL” it sounds without a window.

Update the gateway

Update the firmware of the gateway from the app over Bluetooth — when it is offered, the conditions, the three stages and what each result means.

The Hectare app carries the firmware for the gateway. When the app holds a newer version than the gateway runs, it offers the update. The update goes over Bluetooth and takes about 3 minutes. The gateway writes the new firmware into a spare part of its memory; the working firmware stays untouched and keeps working until the new one has proved itself. Your machine settings (the row profile) are not erased by the update.

Open “Settings” › “Machine” › “Bridge” › “Bridge update”. The row “Bridge update” shows at its right the version the app can install; empty means there is nothing to install. The page shows “Version in the bridge” and “Version in the app”.

The page says Meaning
“Update the bridge to” and a version An update is offered.
“The bridge has the newest version” Nothing to do.
“The bridge is not connected” Connect first: First connection to the gateway.
“The bridge cannot be updated over Bluetooth” This gateway cannot take an update from the app. Ask Agriwelt.

The app offers only firmware made for this gateway board, and only a version newer than the one the gateway runs. Anything else is not offered at all.

Run the update

  1. Stop the machine. Keep the gateway powered and your device — tablet or phone — close to it.

  2. Tap “Update the bridge to”. The conditions appear under “Before the update”: “The bridge is powered, the device is near it.” · “The machine stands: do not seed during the update.” · “Takes about 3 minutes.” · “Do not leave the app or lock the screen.”

  3. Tap “Start the update” (or “Cancel”). Three stages follow: “Firmware transfer” with a percentage, “Bridge restart”, “Checking the new firmware”. Do not leave the app and do not walk away with the device. The screen does not dim and the back button is hidden until the result appears.

  4. The result appears with its code, for example “804 · Bridge updated”. Tap “Done”.

The whole update takes 2 to 2.5 minutes: the transfer 1 to 1.5 minutes (57 seconds on an iPhone XS, 95 seconds on an iPad), the restart 12 to 25 seconds, the check about 25 seconds.

After the restart

About 20 seconds after the new firmware starts, the gateway checks itself — its radio, the sensor line, its settings and its device description — and keeps the new firmware only if everything is in order. If the check fails, the app restarts the gateway once more after 50 seconds and it returns to the previous firmware (code 805); this takes about 2.5 minutes. If no device with the app is near at that moment, the gateway keeps working on the unconfirmed firmware until it is switched off and on, and then returns to the previous one by itself.

During the update the rows are not watched: the app raises no “Bridge link lost” and the buzzer is silent.

The results

Result What to do
804 · “Bridge updated” Nothing. The gateway runs the new firmware and works as before.
805 · “The bridge went back to its previous firmware” The new firmware failed its check; the gateway runs the previous one and works. Tell the service the code.
806 · “The bridge refused the firmware: the signature does not match” Update the app and try again. If it repeats, tell the service the code.
807 · “The bridge refused the firmware: it is not newer” Nothing. The gateway keeps its own firmware.
808 · “Firmware transfer interrupted” Keep the device near the gateway and try again; the transfer starts over.
809 · “The bridge is busy: no update now” or “The machine is seeding: the update did not start” Finish seeding, clear the active alarm, wait a minute and try again.
810 · “The bridge did not come back after the restart” Check the gateway's power, wait a minute. Not back — switch its power off and on and open this page again.

How service codes work

What the three digits of an Agrigate service code mean, how the place is shown beside it, the four classes and how each looks in the app, and how to find a code from the words on the screen.

Every alarm, fault and notable event of an Agrigate system has a service code of three digits. A code never changes its meaning: if you tell the service “102 on row 7”, they know what happened without seeing the screen.

The three digits

The first digit is the part of the system; the other two are the kind of fault.

Code Part of the system
1xx The sensor of a row itself
2xx The seeding of a row — the sensor works, the row sows wrong
3xx The devices on the HUB-8: hopper, fan, metering unit, tractor inputs
4xx Tramline actuators
5xx Not used in this version
6xx The gateway and the link to it
7xx The iPad or iPhone
8xx Updating the gateway
9xx The gateway's answers when the app sets something up

The place is shown beside the code

The code says what; the place says where. It is written beside the code, not in it: “102 · row 7”, “301 · hopper”, “404 · tramline actuator #2”. So the same code covers every row and every device.

The four classes

Alarm

A window with a red title strip “ALARM!” and the buzzer. Something on the machine needs you now. The alarm window

Device fault

The same window with an orange title strip, and the buzzer. A device reports that it is faulty — clogged, light struck, the wrong kind of device.

Attention

No window. A yellow capsule or a line next to what you are doing — for example “Volume is off”, or a message on the setup page. Read it and act when you can. Messages over the screen

Record

Nothing to do. The event is kept for the service.

Finding a code from the screen

NOTE

In this pre-release version the alarm window shows the words of the alarm, not its code. Find the words in the code topics — every code there gives the words the app shows. The update of the gateway shows its codes on screen, for example “804 · Bridge updated”. For the setup answers of the gateway the app shows a number after the words; see 9xx.

Two codes share their words on screen: 201 and 202 both say “Under allowable rate”. The bar tells them apart — yellow for 201, dark red blinking for 202 — and so does the row menu (“Not seeding” for 202).

When you call the service

Give the code (or the words on the screen), the place — the row or the device —, and from “Settings”: the version of the app (“App” › “Version”) and the firmware of the gateway (“Machine” › “Bridge firmware”).

1xx · Row sensor faults

Codes 101–106: a seed or fertilizer sensor of a row is silent, blocked, dazzled, faulty or is not a seed sensor.

Codes 1xx belong to the sensor of a row itself: the sensor does not answer, or it reports that it cannot count. The place is the row: tell the service, for example, “102 · row 7”. The window title is orange for a device fault and red for an alarm.

How to read a code, the four classes and how each looks in the app: How service codes work.

101 · Row sensor not answering

Class: Alarm · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with “Connection lost”, “Seed sensor:” or “Fertilizer sensor:” and the row numbers. The bar of the row shows a red ✕ on a grey background. When several rows lose the link, their numbers join one window.

What to do:

  1. Check the plug and the cable of the sensor of this row.
  2. If several rows next to each other are silent, look for a broken main cable or lost power before the first of them.
  3. If only this row is silent and its cable is good, replace the sensor: Number the sensors.

102 · Row sensor clogged

Class: Device fault · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with an orange title, “Clogged” and the row number. The bar of the row keeps showing its level.

What to do:

  1. Stop.
  2. Clean the seed tube and the window of the sensor of this row.

103 · Row sensor light struck

Class: Device fault · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with an orange title, “Light struck” and the row number.

What to do:

  1. Shade the sensor from direct light: check its cover and the position of the seed tube.
  2. If it comes back in the shade too, tell the service the code and the row number.

104 · Row sensor faulty

Class: Device fault · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with an orange title, “Faulty” and the row number.

What to do:

  1. Unplug the sensor and plug it in again.
  2. If the fault stays, replace the sensor (row menu, “Replace the sensor”). Until then you can take the row off control: “Disable control” in the row menu.

105 · Row sensor reports another fault

Class: Device fault · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with an orange title, “Other fault” and the row number.

What to do:

  1. Tell the service the code and the row number.
  2. You can take the row off control (“Disable control” in the row menu) and go on seeding.

106 · Not a seed sensor on this row

Class: Device fault · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with an orange title, “Wrong device type!” and the row number. The bar blinks bright red with a “?”. This alarm comes at once, without the alarm delay.

What to do:

  1. Check that a seed sensor, not another device, is connected for this row.
  2. Correct the numbering: Number the sensors.

2xx · Seeding alarms

Codes 201–207: the sensor works, but the row sows too little, too much, with doubles or skips, into the tramline, or without control.

Codes 2xx mean that the sensor of the row works and the seeding itself is wrong: the cause is on the seeder, not in the electronics. The place is the row.

How to read a code, the four classes and how each looks in the app: How service codes work.

201 · Row below the allowed rate

Class: Alarm · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with “Under allowable rate” and the row numbers. The bar of the row is yellow: below the control line (Flow) or below the tolerance band (Hybrid, Precision).

What to do:

  1. Check the metering unit and the seed tube of this row.
  2. Check that there is seed above the metering unit.

202 · Row not seeding

Class: Alarm · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with “Under allowable rate” and the row numbers — the same words as code 201. The column of the row blinks dark red; the row menu says “Not seeding”. The sensor answers, no seed passes it, while most rows sow.

What to do:

  1. Stop: the row does not sow.
  2. Check the metering unit, its drive and the seed tube of this row.
  3. Check that there is seed in the hopper.

203 · Row above the allowed rate

Class: Alarm · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with “Rate too high” and the row numbers. The bar of the row is blue. Only in the Hybrid and Precision modes.

What to do:

  1. Check the setting of the metering unit of this row.

204 · Too many doubles

Class: Alarm · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with “Too many doubles” and the row numbers. Only in the Precision mode, and only when “Doubles alarm level” is not set to “No”.

What to do:

  1. Check the seed disc, the doubles eliminator and the vacuum of this row.

205 · Too many skips

Class: Alarm · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with “Too many skips” and the row numbers. Only in the Precision mode, and only when “Skips alarm level” is not set to “No”.

What to do:

  1. Check the seed disc and the vacuum of this row.
  2. Check the ground speed: drive slower if it is too high for the disc.

206 · Seeding into the tramline

Class: Alarm · Place: the row.

What you see. The alarm window with “Seeding in tramline” and the row numbers. The column of the row is hatched grey (tramline) and the seed over it is hatched red.

What to do:

  1. Check the shutter or the clutch of the tramline on this row.
  2. If the row should sow, check the tramline rhythm and the actuator settings: Tramline.

207 · Seeding without control

Class: Attention · Place: the row.

What you see. No window. The yellow capsule “NOT UNDER CONTROL” with the rows of the band blinks over the band, and the buzzer sounds. Seed flows in the band, but fewer of its rows sow within the norm than “Share of seeding rows” asks for — for example when the control line or the rate is far from what the rows really sow.

What to do:

  1. Check the control line or the rate against what the rows really sow.
  2. Check the rows that are yellow or dark red: Bar colours.

3xx · Hopper, fan, metering unit and tractor inputs

Codes 301–311: the devices on the HUB-8 hub — hopper, fan, metering unit, work position and tractor inputs.

Codes 3xx come from the devices wired to the HUB-8 hub: hopper level, fan speed, metering unit rotation and the tractor inputs (oil pressure, battery charge, fuel, air filter). The place is the device, for example “301 · hopper”. How the devices are set up: HUB-8 inputs.

How to read a code, the four classes and how each looks in the app: How service codes work.

301 · Hopper empty

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Hopper empty”. The hopper tile shows “Empty” with a red ring. This alarm sounds even when the machine stands.

What to do:

  1. Fill the hopper.
  2. If the hopper is full, check the level sensor and its setting: HUB-8 inputs.

302 · Fan too slow

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Fan speed too low”. The fan tile shows the speed in rpm with a red ring. Only while seeding.

What to do:

  1. Raise the fan speed: hydraulic flow, engine speed.
  2. Check “Min, rpm” and “Pulses per revolution” of the fan: HUB-8 inputs.

303 · Fan too fast

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Fan speed too high”. The fan tile shows the speed in rpm with a red ring.

What to do:

  1. Lower the fan speed.
  2. Check “Max, rpm” and “Pulses per revolution” of the fan: HUB-8 inputs.

304 · Metering unit not turning

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Dispenser not turning”. The metering unit tile shows “Stopped” with a red ring. Only while seeding, after the time set in “Period, s”.

What to do:

  1. Stop: the metering unit does not turn.
  2. Check its drive, the chain and the clutch.
  3. If it does turn, check the shaft sensor and “Period, s”.

305 · Oil pressure low

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Emergency oil pressure”. With an ignition input it sounds only with the ignition on.

What to do:

  1. Stop the engine.
  2. Check the oil level.

306 · Battery not charging

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “No battery charge”. With an ignition input it sounds only with the ignition on.

What to do:

  1. Check the alternator and its belt.

307 · Fuel low

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Low fuel”.

What to do:

  1. Refuel.

308 · Air filter clogged

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Air filter clogged”.

What to do:

  1. Clean or replace the air filter.

309 · Machine device not answering

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Connection lost:” followed by the name of the device, for example the hopper. Its tile has a grey ring.

What to do:

  1. Check the cable of the named device to the HUB-8.
  2. Check the power and the cable of the HUB-8 itself, if all its devices are silent.

310 · Wrong device on a HUB-8 input

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Wrong device type:” followed by the name of the device. Its tile shows “Wrong”.

What to do:

  1. Check that the device is wired to its own input.
  2. Check that the kind of the device is right in the machine's devices: HUB-8 inputs.

311 · Tractor input in alarm

Class: Alarm · Place: the device.

What you see. The alarm window with “Alarm:” followed by the name of the device.

What to do:

  1. Check the device named in the window.

6xx · Gateway and link

Codes 601–602: the Bluetooth link to the gateway is lost, or the gateway is connected but sends nothing.

Codes 6xx are about the gateway and the radio link between the gateway and the iPad or iPhone. While the link is down, every figure on the screen is old: the screen is dimmed and the alarms of the rows wait.

How to read a code, the four classes and how each looks in the app: How service codes work.

601 · Link to the gateway lost

Class: Alarm · Place: none.

What you see. The alarm window with “Bridge link lost”, after the link has been down for 3 seconds. A red capsule in the middle of the screen says what the app is doing, for example “Looking for the bridge…”. The window closes by itself when the gateway answers again.

What to do:

  1. Check that the gateway has power.
  2. Check that the iPad or iPhone is still in the cab, close to the gateway.
  3. Wait: the app reconnects by itself. If it does not, use “Reconnect” in “Settings” › “Machine” › “Bridge”.

602 · Gateway silent

Class: Alarm · Place: none.

What you see. The red capsule “Bridge is silent…”: the link is there, but no data comes. The screen is dimmed; after 3 seconds the window “Bridge link lost” opens as for code 601.

What to do:

  1. Wait 10 seconds.
  2. If it stays, switch the power of the gateway off and on.

7xx · iPad and iPhone

Codes 701–705: the tablet itself — volume, headphones, GNSS speed and Bluetooth.

Codes 7xx are about the iPad or iPhone that runs the Hectare app: things the tablet does that would hide an alarm or stop the app from reaching the gateway.

How to read a code, the four classes and how each looks in the app: How service codes work.

701 · Tablet volume off

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. The yellow capsule “Volume is off” at the top of the screen and in the alarm window. The media volume of the tablet is below 20 %: the buzzer runs, but nobody hears it.

What to do:

  1. Turn the volume of the tablet up with its volume buttons. The side switch does not silence the alarm; the volume does.

702 · Sound goes to headphones

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. The yellow capsule “Sound in headphones”: the sound goes to headphones, a Bluetooth speaker, AirPlay or a car system, not to the tablet's own speaker.

What to do:

  1. Disconnect the headphones or the external audio so that the buzzer plays from the tablet.

703 · No GNSS speed

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. The words “no speed” where the speed is shown (the speed sheet, “GNSS speed” in “Settings” › “Machine” › “Bridge”). No fresh speed for 2 seconds: Hybrid and Precision cannot judge the rows. No window opens.

What to do:

  1. Move out under the open sky.
  2. Check that the app may use the location: iPad Settings › Privacy & Security › Location Services › Hectare.
  3. Check that this iPad has GNSS: Hybrid and Precision need an iPad with GNSS (Cellular).

704 · Bluetooth off

Class: Alarm · Place: none.

What you see. The red capsule “Bluetooth is off” in the middle of the screen. The app cannot reach the gateway.

What to do:

  1. Switch Bluetooth on in the tablet's Control Centre or Settings.

705 · Bluetooth not allowed

Class: Alarm · Place: none.

What you see. The red capsule “No Bluetooth permission” in the middle of the screen. The app may not use Bluetooth and cannot reach the gateway.

What to do:

  1. Allow Bluetooth for the app: iPad Settings › Hectare › Bluetooth.

8xx · Gateway update

Codes 801–810: the steps and the results of updating the gateway's firmware from the app.

Codes 8xx belong to the update of the gateway's firmware from the app. The update screen shows the code in front of the result, for example “804 · Bridge updated”. How to run the update: Update the gateway.

How to read a code, the four classes and how each looks in the app: How service codes work.

801 · Update transfer started

Class: Record · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen this is the start of the stage “Firmware transfer”; no code is shown. The gateway keeps it as a record.

What to do. Nothing. This code is a record for the service.

802 · Update transferred in full

Class: Record · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen this is the end of the stage “Firmware transfer”, after which the screen moves on to “Bridge restart”; no code is shown. The gateway keeps it as a record. The image is transferred and accepted; its signature is checked later, when the gateway switches to it, and a refusal then comes as 806.

What to do. Nothing. This code is a record for the service.

803 · Update activated

Class: Record · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen this is the stage “Bridge restart”; no code is shown. The gateway keeps it as a record of its restart onto the new firmware.

What to do:

  1. Wait up to a minute and do not switch the gateway off.

804 · Gateway updated

Class: Record · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen: “804 · Bridge updated” and “The new firmware passed its check and runs. The monitor works as before.”

What to do. Nothing. This code is a record for the service.

805 · Gateway went back to the previous firmware

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen: “805 · The bridge went back to its previous firmware”. The new firmware failed its own check; the gateway runs the previous one and works.

What to do:

  1. Go on working.
  2. Tell the service the code.

806 · Firmware refused: signature

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen: “806 · The bridge refused the firmware: the signature does not match”. The gateway keeps its firmware.

What to do:

  1. Update the Hectare app and try again.
  2. If it repeats, tell the service the code.

807 · Firmware refused: not newer

Class: Record · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen: “807 · The bridge refused the firmware: it is not newer” and “The bridge runs its own firmware. Nothing to do.”

What to do. Nothing. This code is a record for the service.

808 · Update transfer interrupted

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen: “808 · Firmware transfer interrupted”. The gateway runs its previous firmware.

What to do:

  1. Keep the tablet close to the gateway.
  2. Start the update again; the transfer starts from the beginning.

809 · Update not possible now

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen: “809 · The bridge is busy: no update now”, or “809 · The machine is seeding: the update did not start”. Nothing was sent.

What to do:

  1. Finish seeding and stop the machine.
  2. Clear the active alarm on the seeder.
  3. Wait a minute and try again.

810 · Gateway did not come back after the restart

Class: Attention · Place: none.

What you see. On the update screen: “810 · The bridge did not come back after the restart”. The app waited 2 minutes for the gateway after its restart.

What to do:

  1. Check the power of the gateway and wait a minute.
  2. If it is still not back, switch the power of the gateway off and on and open the update screen again.
